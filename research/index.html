Research

My research examines selective vaporization of lunar regolith under laser heating, including mass-loss behavior, compositional changes, and process energy demand.

01 Core research

Laser-driven vaporization

How lunar materials respond to intense heating

Controlled laser-heating experiments on lunar regolith simulants investigate temperature-dependent mass loss and material response at reduced pressure.

  • Continuous-wave fiber laser experiments
  • Gravimetric mass-loss measurements
  • Controlled temperatures and ambient pressure

02 Lunar resource processing

Selective oxide enrichment

Beneficiation ahead of metal extraction

Preferential volatilization may change the oxide composition of the residue. The work investigates enrichment of aluminium-bearing material as a possible pre-treatment step.

  • Volatile oxide removal
  • Residue composition and material yield
  • Potential integration with downstream electrolysis

03 Experimental methods

Temperature and plume diagnostics

Measuring processes at very high temperature

Optical spectroscopy and two-color pyrometry are used to monitor the laser-heated target, supporting repeatable processing and interpretation of vaporization behavior.

  • Spectroscopic measurements
  • Two-color temperature estimation
  • Temperature feedback and experiment logging

04 Numerical modelling

Vaporization and energy modelling

Connecting experiments with process feasibility

Reduced-order models explore constituent depletion, evolving material yield, and the role of thermal losses in laser-based beneficiation.

  • Temperature-dependent vaporization rates
  • Compositional evolution and residue yield
  • Energy demand and radiation losses
